V8.2 POWER AMENDMENT: MATCHED FIXED-GROUP ACCEPTANCE CURVES
=======================================================

Status and provenance
---------------------
This is a small additional simulation of acceptance power at the operative
per-group level. The design is frozen in protocol_v8_2.json under
power_extension, SHA-256
6695e33de9eb88ccf74726d3015bc7cc4b2baf650cdb4cb1b2696f3c04f53516.

mapping, interval methods and execution timestamp BEFORE generating orders.
It does not modify or replace the historical simulation.

The original safeguard-validity experiment used 78 finite populations:
N in {50,100,300}, zero-gain fractions {0,.4}, requested mean gains from -.2 to

.05. Its population-construction source is
experiments/safeguard_validity/run_safeguard_validity.py in the historical

49453e74f52b47f75f854c24d55652871b72c83279fcd46df949167dba66f2d1.

Those historical rates are illustrative single-group-level results. They must
not be presented as the power of the maximum-size ten-group shortlist.

Question and scope
------------------
How much does changing only the nominal group level from .05 to .005 alter
the corrected remainder rule's probability of accepting a fixed candidate
group under the same audit-order distribution and the same stopping rules?

The .005 level equals alpha_repair/10 when all ten shortlist positions are
occupied. Actual shorter shortlists use alpha_repair/g, not automatically
.005. The new experiment supplies each isolated group its full per-group
allowance; it omits shortlist ranking, competition for the shared 196-audit
group budget, previous-group outcomes, warm-up, generator selection and final
PAC calibration. It is therefore an operating-characteristic comparison of
fixed group tests, not whole-policy operational power or expected savings.

Design fixed before simulation
------------------------------
Thirty populations cross N={50,100,300}, zero-gain fraction={0,.4}, and
requested mean gain={0,.1,.2,.3,.4}. Gains d belong to {-1,0,+1}. Construction
exactly preserves the original integer rounding rule:

  n_zero = round(zero_fraction*N)
  n_nonzero = N - n_zero
  n_positive = clip(round((requested_gain*N+n_nonzero)/2), 0, n_nonzero)
  n_negative = n_nonzero - n_positive.

Actual mean gain is reported from these counts. In particular, at N=50,
requested .1 and .3 produce .12 and .28. The target and realised means are
different columns; the figure uses the realised mean.

Each population receives 1,000 independent uniform permutations. The two
levels see the same complete order, but can stop at different times. The seed
is fixed at 20261108. Each population gets a separate generator from
SeedSequence([20261108,population_id]); population IDs follow the nested order
N, then zero fraction, then requested gain. The execution record fixes this
mapping before generation. The script archives every permutation hash and
both resulting stopping states.

At each observation, the corrected production WorLowerCS receives x=(d+1)/2
with its 401-point grid and bet cap c=.5. Its exact dual-grid remainder gate
is checked first. If no acceptance occurs, the run stops for futility at
t>=10 and mean(x)<.45, or at min(80,ceil(N/2)) observations. No adaptive
extension of the simulation count, tuning of bets, or selection of plotted
populations is permitted. The negative-gain validity fixtures remain in the
prior verification evidence rather than being rerun as an additional study.

End points and uncertainty
--------------------------
For each fixed population and alpha, report accepted, beneficial accepted
(true unaudited-remainder gain>0), and nonbeneficial accepted (gain<=0), each
as counts and frequencies over all 1,000 orders. Also report audit counts,
stopping reasons and true remainder gains at acceptance. Conditional summaries
are explicitly labelled; zero acceptances leave them undefined, not zero.

Acceptance intervals are pointwise, equal-tailed, two-sided 95% exact binomial
(Clopper-Pearson) intervals. They quantify only Monte Carlo uncertainty about
the fixed population and algorithm. They are not simultaneous over the
population grid, not inference about future annotation panels, and not proof
of the procedure's guarantee.

Changing alpha only changes rejection boundaries. With identical paths and
identical observation-dependent futility/caps, acceptance at .005 implies
acceptance at .05 no later. The runner checks this nesting for every order.
Consequently the paired difference p(.005)-p(.05) is minus the probability
of a .05-only acceptance. Its 95% interval is [-upper,-lower] from the exact
binomial interval for that paired lost-acceptance indicator. The two levels
must not be treated as independent simulations.

The complete design is plotted, including zero-gain points. Any nonbeneficial
acceptances are reported as observed counts, not suppressed or treated as a
failed requirement merely because a finite-sample count is nonzero. The
mathematical false-certification control remains a probabilistic statement;
this small power extension is not an additional proof of that guarantee.

Outputs and rerun
-----------------
  python3 scripts/run_operational_power.py --workers 2

Outputs are results/power_execution_freeze.json, power_populations.csv,
power_trials.csv, power_population_summary.csv, power_paired_summary.csv,
power_acceptance_curves.pdf/png, power_summary.txt and power_manifest.json.
The source/input/output hashes are checked before returning a cached run.
For a distinct reproduction, supply --out results/power_reproduction.

The simulation calls the publication implementation. It is a matched design
and a rerunnable computational experiment; it is not an independently
implemented confidence-sequence solver or an independent annotation sample.

Completed execution
-------------------
The prespecified run completed on 4 November 2026: all 30 populations, 30,000
matched audit orders and 60,000 level-specific runs. The protocol and source
hashes stayed unchanged throughout. Every paired acceptance satisfied the
expected alpha nesting. See power_manifest.json for complete hashes and the
execution environment, and the CSV files for all outcomes.

Illustrative slices from the COMPLETE plotted grid, with 40% zero-gain items:

  N     actual mean gain     acceptance at .05     acceptance at .005
  50          .4                    5.7%                    0.0%
  100         .4                   62.4%                    7.5%
  300         .4                   97.5%                   85.0%

Each percentage is based on 1,000 orders. The 0/1,000 case has a pointwise
two-sided 95% exact upper endpoint of 0.3682%; zero observed acceptances do
not establish zero acceptance probability. At N=100 and mean gain .4, the
paired decrease is 54.9 percentage points; the CSV supplies its exact paired
interval. The two level-specific acceptance rates are not independent.

Nonbeneficial accepted remainders occurred in the simulation and are retained
in the output: 67 at .05 and five at .005 across the heterogeneous grid, with
counts and exact intervals per population. These totals are inventory counts,
not a pooled operational error probability or evidence of a zero-error rule.
The extension supplies the previously missing level comparison; it does not
convert a Monte Carlo result into a proof or estimate the full policy's power.
